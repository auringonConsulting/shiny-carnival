For government

A new initiative lands on your agency: a rule to put in place, a grant to deploy, a program leadership wants launched. It’s too big to add to someone’s existing job and too short-lived to hire for. I’m a management consultant who takes it from approved to running, then hands it to your staff.

Where it starts

What agencies call about

The deadline. A rule or mandate has a date attached, and the program behind it doesn’t exist yet.

The grant. The funding came through, and now it has to turn into something residents can use.

The pilot. It worked at small scale, and nobody has had time to make it permanent.

The workaround. Staff are patching over a process or system that was never designed for what they’re doing now.

The partners. The initiative depends on outside organizations or companies, and they need to be brought along.

Approach

How this works

Understand

Typically 3–4 weeks.

I start with what’s actually happening. I look at the data you already have and talk to the staff, residents, and partners it affects, so the design starts from evidence. You get a written assessment of where things stand and what matters most.

Design

Typically 6–8 weeks.

I shape how the program will run: who it serves, who does what, the process behind it, and the systems that support it. That covers the technical and the non-technical, from data and tools to eligibility rules, staffing, and partner agreements. You get a concept of operations and an implementation plan.

Stand it up

Typically 3–6 months.

I get it running: redesigning the processes, setting up the tools, coordinating partners, and training staff as we go. You get a working program, with documented procedures and a team that knows how to run it.

Hand it off

The engagement ends when your team runs it without me, with an end date set before we start.

If the initiative is bigger than one person, I can bring in a team I trust, and I stay your point of contact.

The work

Where this comes from

As Director of Analytics and later Chief Experience and Transformation Officer at NYC’s Taxi & Limousine Commission, I built programs and the teams to run them.

Turning a rule into wheelchair-accessible rides

NYC’s for-hire regulator required every company, from car services to Uber and Lyft, to provide wheelchair-accessible service, with a deadline and nothing yet to run it. I designed the program that made it work and brought the industry along. Accessible trips grew from under 7,000 to nearly 140,000 a month.

Building a team, and the agency’s first product practice

I was hired to run analytics and left behind a team of ten across data, product, and operations. When we pooled the agency’s four customer service channels, we found people bouncing between units for weeks on a single issue. We fixed it with single-owner cases and better routing, without restructuring the agency, and wait times dropped. The team also built the distribution methodology for the Medallion Relief Fund, the city’s response to driver debt. The work made the case for a new seat: the agency’s first chief product officer. I later returned as Chief Experience and Transformation Officer to lead change management for the agency’s technology modernization, serving more than 225,000 licensees.
