The work

What the work looks like

The board conversation that keeps circling

A portfolio company misses two quarters, and every board meeting relitigates the last one. You introduce me; the founder takes the call. I spend two weeks in the work: with the team, in the numbers, across everything between roadmap and release. At the end you’re both holding the same document: where delivery is stuck, the fixes, who on the team owns each.

The program behind its enrollment number

A foundation funds a benefits navigation program, and enrollment is trailing the proposal. The program officer suspects intake; the team wants the help. I embed with the team, rebuild intake around the data they already collect, and train the staff who run it. The capability stays with the grantee.

The gap that repeats

A company in the portfolio is drowning in support. The fix lands: automated answers over the platform’s own data, judgment calls routed to a human. Months later a different team hits the same wall. That introduction takes one sentence, and the build goes faster the second time.

The first call

Two questions: which team is on your mind, and what hasn’t shown up yet. Thirty minutes, no pitch. If it’s not a fit, I’ll say so, and point you to someone who can help.
